My goal is to become an engineer who makes useful technology affordable for everyone. My first proof: an air purifier that works like a ₹40,000 one, built for ₹2,000.

A bit about me

I'm a Grade 9 IGCSE student in India. I like turning ideas into real, working things: I design my own parts, 3D print them, wire my own circuits and keep testing until they work.

Away from the workbench, you'll find me in a Model UN committee room, on a football pitch or a basketball court, in the pool, or practising the violin and the drums. Each of these teaches me something the others can't. Football taught me how to lead a team under pressure. Debate taught me to understand the other side before I answer it. Music taught me patience and precision.

That mix is the point. The problems I care about, like clean air, don't belong to one subject, so I don't want to belong to only one either.

Projects

Things I've designed and built myself, documented step by step.

Low-Cost, Efficient Air Purifier

Branded purifiers in India cost ₹11,000 to ₹69,000, but the part that cleans the air is just the filter. I bought a real HEPA + carbon filter and a fan, and designed and printed everything else myself.

₹2,000total cost
13 Wfan power
360°air intake
See how I built it
Clean air shouldn't cost ₹40,000.

What drives me is simple: good technology is often locked behind a high price. The expensive part of a product is rarely the part that does the work. If I can find that part, I can rebuild the rest cheaply and share how.

I bring the same idea to teams. Leading isn't about having a title. It's about stepping up when the room needs someone, and making sure everyone around you can do their best.

What's next: a washable fine-mesh pre-filter for the purifier, which I'm adding now so the main filter lasts longer.
